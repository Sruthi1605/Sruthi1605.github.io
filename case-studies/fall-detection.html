Fall Detection System

A deep learning system that detects and categorizes human falls from RGB camera video — 87% accuracy on the LE2I dataset.

Featured · Deep Learning 2025 Computer Vision
  • Python
  • PyTorch
  • YOLOv5
  • ResNet50

01. Overview

Falls are one of the leading causes of injury for older adults, and the damage worsens the longer help takes to arrive. The goal was a vision system that watches live video, detects the moment of a fall, and tells a caregiver how severe it is.

I trained a detection + classification pipeline that takes RGB video frames and outputs both whether a fall happened and its severity level.

"The aim wasn't just to notice a fall — it was to categorize it, so responders know how urgent the situation is before they arrive."

02. The Problem

  • Falls are rare, fast, and visually similar to normal activities (sitting down, bending over).
  • Cameras only give RGB video — no depth, skeleton joints, or wearables to fall back on.
  • Automated monitors must not overwhelm caretakers with false alarms.
  • Severity varies — a slip differs from a collapse — and responders need to prioritize.

03. My Role & Approach

  • Used YOLOv5 for person detection and tracking across frames to isolate the subject.
  • Extracted motion and posture features and passed them through ResNet50 for fall classification.
  • Trained on the LE2I fall dataset with augmentations to handle real-world variability.
  • Built multi-class output that separates falls by severity: mild, moderate, severe.

04. Key Challenges

  • Ambiguity: distinguishing "falling" from "lying down" required temporal context, not single frames.
  • Imbalance: fall samples were sparse relative to normal activity — used data balancing and augmentation.
  • Severity grading: mapped continuous movement patterns into three clinically meaningful buckets.

05. Results & Impact

  • Achieved 87% accuracy on the LE2I dataset.
  • Classified fall severity into mild, moderate, and severe.
  • Proved the approach works on plain RGB video — no wearables or special sensors required.
